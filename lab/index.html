📖 how the lab works — the mystery, the method, and how it revers the map

S1 — Sequential, one-pixel seed: "the game derives its ENTIRE random stream from ONE world pixel's coordinates (the fill's seeding origin — corner? center? +1?). That single stream then colors ALL informative vertices of the whole 5×2-chunk lab area (one continuous lattice, one fill call)." The pixel itself is unknown and the mixer avalanches (no gradient), so the engine tries EVERY pixel of the area as that origin: ~2.6M candidates × draw shapes × alignments.
S2 — Sequential, world-seed stream: "the stream comes from SetRandomFromWorldSeed(worldSeed), but an unknown number of draws were consumed first." Engine tries skip 0..200k.
P — Positional: "no stream — each vertex's color is hashed directly from its coordinates" (the game's SetRandomSeed pattern), across coordinate systems × offsets × draw shapes.
depth (x/389) — informative vertices matched before the first mismatch. Wrong candidates die at 1–2; the true rule walks 389 clean. The dashed line is the luckiest-random-candidate expectation (~log2N) — only a trend above it is signal.
reading P bests — P candidates are scored on all 389 vertices at once (a percentage); their "depth" display is just pct × 3.89. The current 60%-ish P bests are the expected luckiest-of-28k scores, not signal. "exhausted" = every candidate in the selected templates was tested — a completed elimination, not an error. The grid canvas shows diagonal vertex classes (grey = seed-independent), with holes from two lab chunks that never generated.
