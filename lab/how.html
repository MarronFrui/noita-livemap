How the Wang Lab works — and why it revers Noita's map generation

← back to the search supervisor

1 · The mystery: one box is missing

Noita's world is a function of its seed. Everything else in that function is already reconstructed and verified against the running game — except one step:

The map is painted with herringbone wang tiles: a lattice of vertices sits over the terrain; each vertex gets a color from a small palette; each placed tile must have its six corner colors match the vertices it covers. The tile-placing algorithm is fully decoded. What nobody knows is how the game decides each vertex's color. That decision is the entire difference between "a Noita-looking cave" and "this exact cave for this exact seed".

2 · Why the lab exists: observability

To test a candidate rule you need the correct answer to compare against. Two obvious sources both fail:

SourceProblem
Screenshots of the real mapThe rendered world is ~24 points denser than any raw tile layer — walls, pixel scenes and edge fills paint over the wang data. Tile colors are consumed at generation (measured: 0/32 spawn colors visible in captures). Geometry comparisons can never prove or disprove a fill rule.
Item / entity positions in real biomesSpawn facts are exact but sparse (~40–70 per seed) and only constrain a handful of lattice cells.

The lab removes both limits: it injects a custom 16-tile biome into the real game as a 5×2-chunk rectangle. Every tile's pixels carry a unique marker color, and the game itself calls our script for every placed pixel. The game therefore writes out its own complete answer key: which of the 16 tiles it placed, at every lattice cell, for any seed we force.

3 · The search: candidates, accuracy, noise

A candidate is a complete story of how the vertex colors are produced. The engine simulates the story with the game-verified RNG and walks the lattice in generation order:

S1 — sequential, one-pixel seed. The game seeds one stream at world pixel (sx, sy), then colors vertices by drawing in order. The mixer avalanches, so there is no gradient to climb — the engine enumerates every pixel of the lab area (~2.6M × draw shapes × alignments ≈ 94M candidates).
S2 — sequential, world-seed stream. Same, but the stream comes from SetRandomFromWorldSeed after an unknown number of pre-consumed draws (skip 0..200k, advanced incrementally).
P — positional. No stream: each vertex's color is hashed directly from its coordinates (the game's own SetRandomSeed(x,y) pattern), across coordinate systems × offsets × draw shapes × warm-up/multi-draw variants.
Miss budget — the S sweeps can tolerate a fraction of mismatches, to catch schemes that legally re-color some vertices (the library's "repetition reduction" pass).

Accuracy = matched informative vertices ÷ 389. A wrong candidate's accuracy collapses at its first contradiction, so the engine aborts it within a few draws — that is why millions of candidates per minute are possible. The trap this dashboard guards against: over millions of candidates, pure luck produces impressive-looking accuracies (the noise floor — drawn on the trend chart). Only a candidate at or near 100% is a discovery; a family finishing below it is a completed elimination.

4 · How this actually gets us the map

  1. Elimination is progress. Every exhausted family is a branch of possibilities proven empty (already dead: streams seeded anywhere in the area, world-seed skips ≤200k, direct position hashes in 7 shapes, per-chunk lattices). The space of possible answers is finite and shrinking — and the structural facts learned along the way (global continuous lattice, per-vertex seed-avalanched values, binary class structure) are exactly the constraints a deeper analysis needs.
  2. A hit is the rule. If a candidate walks all 389 vertices clean on seed 91 and verifies on 92/93, we have the coloring law. We then replay it on real biomes and validate against the spawn-equation judge (paint-immune, seeds 91/92/93).
  3. If everything cheap is exhausted, the lab has earned the expensive tool: targeted decompilation (Ghidra) of the fill function — with the search rig kept as the instant verification bench for whatever the decompilation reveals.
  4. End state: vertex rule + decoded tile placement = the world as a pure function of the seed → the livemap renders any seed's terrain live, no captures.

5 · Operating it

Dump sessions (game required): enable noita-wang-lab + your seed mod only; force a seed; new game; the mod auto-visits the lab rect (~35 s); quit. One CSV per seed appears in save00. Repeat per seed. Then run node mapgen/experiments/2026-09-04-lab-vertex-recovery.mjs to rebuild the answer key.

Search sessions (no game needed): this dashboard, or headless: node --max-old-space-size=4096 mapgen/experiments/2026-09-04-lab-optimizer.mjs 60.

Resources: search RSS ≈ 72 MB, single-thread ~50–300k candidates/s; artifacts on the repo disk (~350 MB, gitignored); the game disk only holds the small CSVs.

Status snapshot (2026-09-04 night): S1, S2 and P exhausted with accuracies at or below their noise floors (best positional candidate 60.41% = expected luckiest-of-28k). Miss-budget sweeps (rep-reduction tolerance) and further template families are the open queue; targeted decompilation is the sanctioned next escalation if they stay empty.
